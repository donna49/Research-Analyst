The constituent elements of common law negligent misrepresentation investment claims are:[^n]

(a) duty of care based on a special relationship between the plaintiff and the defendant;

(b) an untrue, inaccurate, or misleading representation;

(c) the defendant making the representation negligently;

(d) the plaintiff having reasonably relied on the misrepresentation; and,

(e) the plaintiff suffering damages as a consequence of relying on the misrepresentation.

With the benefit of discovery, the Plaintiff may be able to add a cause of action for common law fraud,[^n] which eliminates reliance as a constituent element to alleging that the defendant's fraud resulted in the plaintiff to act and suffer an injury:[^n]

(a) the defendant made a false representation;

(b) the defendant had some level of knowledge of the falsehood of the representation;

(c) the false representation caused the plaintiff to act;[^n] and

(d) the plaintiff's actions resulted in a loss.

The constituent elements of the Ontario Securities Act, Part XXIII.1, s. 138.3 are:[^n]

(a) a responsible issuer, or an individual acting with its actual or apparent authority, disseminates a document that includes a misrepresentation;

(b) the investor acquired the security after the document's issuance but prior to the public correction of the misrepresentation; and

(c) the claimant possesses a right of action for damages against the issuer and its directors, among other statutory defendants.
