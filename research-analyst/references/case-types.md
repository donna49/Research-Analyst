# Case-type playbooks

Classify each case at stage 3 (issue definition). A case can belong to more than one type. Each
playbook lists: what to collect beyond the standard set, the theory pattern that Andrew used, the
evidence that decided his past cases, the adverse precedent to raise in Bear v. Bull, and the
exemplar memo in `corpus.md` to read before drafting.

## 1. Guidance cut / earnings miss after a reaffirmation
- **Collect:** every guidance figure and every reaffirmation, with dates; how far into the
  reporting period each reaffirmation came (e.g. "five weeks into Q3"); the analyst Q&A on the
  subject; before-and-after guidance tables (Paladin: all metrics other than production
  "Retracted"); segment or region drivers that went undisclosed (FiServ: Argentina's share of
  organic growth).
- **Theory:** the guidance rested on assumptions that were unreasonable given facts already known,
  so the forward-looking safe harbour does not apply (Barrick); omitted drivers (FiServ).
- **Decisive evidence:** a later admission ("the biggest factor … was Argentina"); analyst notes
  saying a fact had not been disclosed before; internal data timelines; insider sales.
- **Bear v. Bull:** forward-looking safe harbour; the cautionary language attached to each figure;
  *Smith v. Gap* / Peloton for retail.
- **Exemplars:** FiServ II, Paladin, Barrick DD.

## 2. Mining or industrial project: construction, ramp-up, production
- **Collect:** feasibility studies and NI 43-101 reports; capex and schedule promises; "fully
  funded" statements; mill throughput or production data; contractor lawsuits and builders' liens;
  covenant waivers; impairment tests; later affidavits by officers (CCAA or lien proceedings).
- **Theory:** "fully funded" and "commercial production in Qx" statements, contradicted by facts
  that existed at the time (Ascot); a resource estimate that was a misstatement of past fact, not a
  failed forecast (Silvercorp); material changes to the resource base (OSC SN 51-722).
- **Decisive evidence:** the CEO's own affidavit; lien registrations with dates and amounts; the
  date drift table ("CURIOUS"); material change reports that were never filed.
- **Bear v. Bull:** *Mask v. Silvercorp* (leave denied); the forward-looking safe harbour.
- **Exemplars:** Ascot, Global Atomic, Silvercorp appeal memo, Pretium NI memo.

## 3. Conditional financing (government or strategic loan, offtake)
- **Collect:** the conditions attached to the loan or commitment (DOE, DFC, EDC); every promised
  approval date; the lender's own agendas, board minutes and process timelines; peer letters of
  interest; FOIA routes; geopolitical events.
- **Theory:** omission of the pre-conditions; a serially slipping approval date presented as on
  track (Global Atomic, Li-Cycle).
- **Decisive evidence:** independent agency records showing the application was never on the
  agenda; a timing contradiction in the company's own excuse.
- **Bear v. Bull:** act of state or geopolitical shock as a complete defence. Neutralize it by
  framing the claim on the company's own loan, schedule and cost statements.
- **Exemplars:** Global Atomic (both memos), Li-Cycle.

## 4. Contract, counterparty or project termination
- **Collect:** the contract's history and milestones; the counterparty's statements; local and
  foreign press; auditor changes; earnings-call avoidance of the topic.
- **Theory:** "How does a Company … 2 years into a 3 year contract abruptly walk away? Surely, the
  Company should have known for several months." Use the correction's own words as an admission
  ("a pattern of legal, social, and security obstacles").
- **Exemplar:** Canacol.

## 5. Dividend / distribution cut or suspension
- **Collect:** every distribution declaration and payout-ratio statement ("a high level of
  certainty that the distribution will be maintained"); special committee or strategic review
  timing; related-party or management conflicts; any take-over proposal.
- **Theory:** a decision was made or contemplated before the last statement on the dividend.
  Possible oppression where insiders benefit (Slate).
- **Decisive evidence:** the correction's own words ("not taken in response to a change in the
  underlying fundamentals"); special-committee timeline; drop far exceeding the dividend's value.
- **Bear v. Bull:** *Coffin v. Atlantic Power* (leave denied; Andrew walked away from it after his
  own due diligence).
- **Exemplars:** Slate Grocery; Atlantic Power cover email.

## 6. Regulatory, enforcement or criminal matter (AML, price-fixing, FDA, investigations)
- **Collect:** subpoenas and investigation disclosures; regulator releases; guilty pleas; congressional
  letters; the FDA briefing documents and advisory-committee votes; settlement amounts.
- **Theory:** known investigations or deficiencies omitted from the disclosure; risk factors
  described as hypothetical after the risk had already happened.
- **Decisive evidence:** a drug-by-drug or issue-by-issue cross-reference table, regulator → MD&A →
  complaint (Endo); dates of regulator correspondence.
- **Exemplars:** Endo (three memos), TD/First Horizon, Intellipharmaceutics FDA file.

## 7. Accident / environmental / operational incident
- **Collect:** official incident bulletins; regulator reports; court affidavits; earlier incidents of
  the same kind; compliance statements made beforehand (safety, ESG reports).
- **Theory:** misstatements about safety-protocol compliance before the incident, or omissions about
  it afterward.
- **Hold rule:** **Monitor** until an official report is published, if the price recovered or if a
  third party broke the chain of causation (Alamos). Analogies: BP (2010), SSR Mining (2024).
- **Exemplars:** Alamos, Barrick DD, BP DD.

## 8. Insider trading, tipping, abnormal trading before the release
- **Collect:** intraday price and volume ahead of the release time stamp; SEDI / Form 4 / PDMR;
  regulator records (AMF, OSC), including those obtained by journalists through FOIA; family and
  associate links; ownership percentages.
- **Theory:** OBCA/ABCA s. 248 oppression seeking disgorgement; misrepresentation claims if the
  insiders' knowledge contradicts what was disclosed.
- **Exemplars:** Terravest, Sintana (both unfinished: draw the method from them, not the text).

## 9. Accounting, restatement, related-party transactions, auditor issues
- **Collect:** restated periods; auditor opinions, fees and changes; related-party definitions (MI
  61-101, CICA 3840 / IFRS IAS 24); customer concentration; short-seller reports.
- **Theory:** auditor liability as an "expert" (s. 138.3(1)(e)); omitted related-party relationships
  (Silvercorp / Yongning).
- **Exemplars:** Silvercorp auditor memo, Aurora (round-trip sales), Wayland.

## 10. M&A: failed deal, approval risk, take-over
- **Collect:** the deal announcement; approval conditions; regulator status; the termination release
  (time stamp); break fee.
- **Theory:** omitted regulatory obstacles that made approval unlikely (TD/First Horizon).
- **Exemplar:** TD/First Horizon.

## 11. Cross-listed foreign issuer / CDRs
- **Collect:** CDR or ADR listing date and ratio; the custodian (CIBC Mellon) and the deposit
  agreement; semi-annual position reports; Canadian subsidiaries, employees and institutional
  holders; prospectus language on Canadian remedies; the US complaint (docket, class period, CWs).
- **Theory:** OSA responsible issuer (*Van Breda*, *Canadian Solar*); a class of CDR purchasers plus
  Canadian residents who bought US-listed shares; a foreign state statute.
- **Strategy:** issue the claim, then wait for the US mediation.
- **Exemplars:** FiServ I–II, the CDR due diligence memo, the Super Micro memos.

## 12. Derivative suit
- **Collect:** leave requirements (Bank Act s. 334, CBCA s. 239, OBCA s. 246); demand letters;
  competing derivative and class actions; limitation dates.
- **Exemplar:** TD derivative (the "crowded" pass).

## 13. Fund NAV / product mispricing
- **Collect:** the US complaint as a template; the Canadian regulatory standard (NI 81-106), which may
  bar the theory; auditor.
- **Exemplar:** Putnam.
