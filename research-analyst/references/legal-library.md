# Legal library: boilerplate and citations Andrew reuses

The text below is copied verbatim from his memos. The source memo is shown in brackets after each
item. The plugin inserts this text into the memo; it never paraphrases or invents a citation.

Two flags appear below:
- **⚠ verify** means the corpus gives this citation in conflicting forms. Check it on CanLII,
  CourtListener or the official reporter before the memo leaves draft, and record that check as a
  ledger `legal` record.
- **(not from corpus)** means the item is standard law that Andrew's memos never stated. It must be
  checked against a primary source before use.

## Canada: Ontario (default forum)

### Common-law negligent misrepresentation [Slate; FiServ I–II; Aurora; Ascot; Global Atomic]
> The constituent elements of common law negligent misrepresentation investment claims are:
> (a) duty of care based on a special relationship between the plaintiff and the defendant;
> (b) an untrue, inaccurate, or misleading representation;
> (c) the defendant making the representation negligently;
> (d) the plaintiff having reasonably relied on the misrepresentation; and,
> (e) the plaintiff suffering damages as a consequence of relying on the misrepresentation.

Footnote: *LBP Holdings Ltd v. Hycroft Mining Corp*, 2020 ONSC 59, at para. 20, and *Stajic v. Ward*,
2026 ONSC 1707, at para. 13.

Older source for the same test: *Queen v. Cognos Inc.*, [1993] 1 S.C.R. 87 [Detour; Reasonable
Investigation memo].

Usual add-on: "This cause of action would be limited to Canadian based investors" when certification
is contested (*Silver v. Imax*) [Alamos; Ascot; TD/First Horizon].

Alberta adds holders' claims: *Mewburn v. Mowbrey Gil LLP*, 2022 ABKB 810 [Aurora].

### Common-law fraud [Slate; FiServ II; Li-Cycle]
> With the benefit of discovery, the Plaintiff may be able to add a cause of action for common law
> fraud, which eliminates reliance as a constituent element to alleging that the defendant's fraud
> resulted in the plaintiff to act and suffer an injury:
> (a) the defendant made a false representation;
> (b) the defendant had some level of knowledge of the falsehood of the representation;
> (c) the false representation caused the plaintiff to act; and
> (d) the plaintiff's actions resulted in a loss.

Footnotes:
- *Bayens v. Kinross Gold Corporation*, 2014 ONCA 901, at para. 134, citing *Carom v. Bre-X
  Minerals Ltd.* (2000), 51 O.R. (3d) 236 (C.A.), leave to appeal to S.C.C. refused.
- *Borrelli v. Chan*, 2018 ONSC 1429, at paras. 911 and 918. From para. 918: "If a misrepresentation is
  obviously material, it is a natural inference that the plaintiff relied on it."

Language rule: the memo pleads fraud only as a possible future claim ("with the benefit of
discovery"). It never states fraud as a finding (`language-rules.md`).

### OSA Part XXIII.1, s. 138.3: newer form [Slate; FiServ I–II]
> The constituent elements of the Ontario Securities Act, Part XXIII.1, s. 138.3 are:
> (a) a responsible issuer, or an individual acting with its actual or apparent authority,
> disseminates a document that includes a misrepresentation;
> (b) the investor acquired the security after the document's issuance but prior to the public
> correction of the misrepresentation; and
> (c) the claimant possesses a right of action for damages against the issuer and its directors,
> among other statutory defendants.

Footnote: *Stajic v. Ward*, 2026 ONSC 1707, at para. 15.

### s. 138.3: pleading form [Aurora; Ascot; Canacol]
> (1) the making of a misrepresentation or the failure to disclose a material fact; (2) a public
> correction of the false information; and (3) the trading of a security between the time of the
> misrepresentation or the failure to disclose a material fact. Thus, to plead the statutory causes
> of action, the plaintiff should: (1) identify the inculpatory statement or omission and when it was
> made or ought to have been made; (2) specify the falseness of the inculpatory statement; and (3)
> identify the public correction (which content must be "linked" to the alleged misrepresentations)
> and when it was made.

### Leave to proceed, s. 138.8 [Silvercorp appeal memo]
- "a relatively low threshold": leave is denied only if "the plaintiffs' case is so weak or has been
  so successfully rebutted by the defendant, that it has no reasonable possibility of success."
  *Green v. CIBC*, 2012 ONSC 3637, paras. 373–374; aff'd 2014 ONCA 90, para. 96. See *CIBC v.
  Green*, 2015 SCC 60, para. 118.
- The plaintiff must "offer both a plausible analysis of the applicable legislative provisions,
  and some credible evidence in support for the claim." *CIBC v. Green*, 2015 SCC 60, para. 121,
  citing *Theratechnologies*, 2015 SCC 18.
- *Mask v. Silvercorp Metals*, 2015 ONSC 5348, para. 38 (Belobaba J.): "does any part of the
  plaintiff's case have a reasonable or realistic chance of success at trial?"
- Strategy: serve the notice of motion for leave together with the claim. Serving it suspends the
  s. 138.14 limitation for class members (*Green v. CIBC*, 2014 ONCA 90) [TD/First Horizon;
  Silvercorp costs memo].

### Reasonable investigation defence, s. 138.4(6)–(7) [Reasonable Investigation memo; Slate]
- Two branches: (1) the defendant conducted or caused a reasonable investigation before release;
  (2) the defendant had no reasonable grounds to believe the document contained a misrepresentation.
  Authorities: *Rahimi v. SouthGobi*, 2017 ONCA 719, para. 43; *Gould v. Western Coal*, 2012 ONSC
  5184, para. 267; *Abdula v. Canadian Solar*, 2014 ONSC 5167, para. 76.
- At the leave stage the test is "whether there is a reasonable possibility that the defendants will
  not be able to establish one or both branches of the reasonable investigation defence"
  (*Wong v. Pretium Resources*, 2017 ONSC 3361 — Andrew's case). Conflicting evidence lets the
  action proceed (*Rahimi*, paras. 34, 75).
- The onus is on the defendants, and the business judgment rule does not apply (*Silver v. Imax*,
  2009 CarswellOnt 7874, para. 376).
- The s. 138.4(7) factors, (a) to (k), must be applied to each individual defendant: the nature of
  the issuer; the person's knowledge, experience and function; office held; any other relationship
  with the issuer; the disclosure-compliance system and reliance on it; the period within which
  disclosure was required; professional standards (experts); knowledge of content and medium; role
  in preparing and releasing the document; and, for timely-disclosure failures, role in the
  decision not to disclose.
- His application of the defence in Slate: "the trustees … had reasonable grounds to believe that
  the Impugned Document contained the misrepresentation". The expert-reliance exception in
  s. 138.4(11) fails if the expert reliance was itself undisclosed.

### Damages window, liability limit, market capitalization
- **s. 138.5 (10-day window):** damages are assessed against the average trading price over the 10
  trading days after the correction [Aurora; Global Atomic: "the ten-day look-back"].
- **s. 138.7 (issuer cap):** the greater of 5% of market capitalization and $1 million
  **(not from corpus: the $1M floor; confirm in the statute at ontario.ca/laws/statute/90s05,
  s. 138.7(1)(a))**. His wording: "there is a liability limit
  measured by 5% of the Company's market capitalization at the commencement of the release of the
  first or each of the Impugned Documents" [Aurora; Ascot].
- **Market capitalization, Ont. Reg. 1015, s. 249** [Pretium market-cap memo]. Steps:
  1. Average the outstanding securities of the class at the close of each of the 10 trading days
     before the misrepresentation.
  2. Multiply by the trading price on the principal market over those 10 trading days.
  3. Add each class with a published market.
  4. Add the fair market value of classes without one.
  `scripts/economics.py` implements this. The HEXO expert report includes warrants and options in
  the cap.

### Limitations
- **s. 138.14:** 3 years from the release of the first impugned document. It is suspended once a
  proposed representative plaintiff pleads s. 138.3 and seeks leave [TD/First Horizon; Silvercorp
  costs memo].
- **Limitations Act, 2002, ss. 4–5:** 2 years from discovery. *Kaynes v. BP PLC*, 2019 ONSC 6464
  [TD derivative].
- **Amendments after a limitation has run:** *Marks v. Ottawa*, [2011] O.J. No. 1445, para. 19;
  *Frohlick v. Pinkerton*, 2008 ONCA, para. 30 (prejudice is presumed) [BP revive memo].

### Certification [Li-Cycle; Certified Secondary Market memo]
- CPA ss. 5(1) and 6. *Fantl v. Transamerica Life Canada*, 2015 ONSC 1367 (Div. Ct.), paras. 44–48.
- *Bayens v. Kinross*, 2014 ONCA 901, paras. 97–98 and 104: denial of leave under the OSA does not
  doom the common-law claims.
- *Brown v. CIBC*, 2012 ONSC 2377, para. 195 (individual assessments do not defeat preferability).
- Common-law secondary-market claims have been certified: *Carom v. Bre-X* (C.A.); *Dobbie v. Arctic
  Glacier*, 2011 ONSC 25; *Ironworkers v. Manulife*, 2013 ONSC 4083 (para. 81); *Swisscanto v.
  BlackBerry*, 2016 ONSC 534; *CIBC v. Green*, 2015 SCC 60 (reliance is not a common issue).

### Oppression, OBCA s. 248 [Slate; Li-Cycle; Colt]
The text of s. 248(2) is quoted in Slate. The analysis has two steps: (1) the reasonable
expectations of the security holder; (2) whether the conduct amounts to oppression, unfair prejudice
or unfair disregard (*BCE Inc., Re*, 2008 SCC 69, via *Rooney v. ArcelorMittal*, 2018 ONSC 1878).
Public statements can ground reasonable expectations (*Ford Motor Co. v. OMERS*, [2006] O.J. No. 27,
para. 66). Li-Cycle uses it to seek disgorgement of insider-trading profits and the appointment of
a monitor.

### Primary market, s. 130 [Global Atomic; Detour underwriters memo]
Each offering in the Class Period gets its own primary-market class. Every s. 130 claim meeting the
technical requirements has been certified. The four failures were procedural (limitations, or a
representative plaintiff who bought only in the secondary market) [Detour].

### Gatekeepers [Detour; Silvercorp auditor memo]
- "expert" (s. 138.1) includes an "accountant, actuary, appraiser, auditor, engineer, financial
  analyst, geologist or lawyer". Liability under s. 138.3(1)(e) requires the misrepresentation to be
  in the expert's report and the expert's written consent.
- Underwriter as gatekeeper (*YBM Magnex (Re)* (2003), 26 OSCB 6285, para. 188, quoting *Re A.E.
  Ames*): "the underwriter must seek out and question all relevant and material facts concerning
  the issuer…". An underwriter is a "promoter" only if it did "something more" than conventional
  banking (*Goldsmith v. National Bank*, 2015 ONSC 2746, paras. 24–34).

### Jurisdiction over foreign or non-Canadian-listed issuers [FiServ I–II; Super Micro; Nevsun; BP]
- *Club Resorts Ltd. v. Van Breda*, 2012 SCC 17 (para. 90 sets out the presumptive connecting factors).
  This is "the same test to make a foreign corporation a 'responsible issuer'".
- *Abdula v. Canadian Solar*: 2011 ONSC 5105, para. 45; 2012 ONCA 211, paras. 10, 39, 49–81, 88;
  2015 ONSC 53. A Nasdaq-only issuer can be a responsible issuer. ⚠ verify the pinpoint
  for each use; the corpus cites different reports for the same point.
- *Paniccia v. MDC Partners*, 2017 ONSC 7298, paras. 4–7: OSA claims can cover US-traded securities
  that are already part of a pending US action.
- *Leon v. Volkswagen AG*, 2018 ONSC 4265, paras. 6, 20, 28, 32. This is the adverse case: VW
  securities bought on foreign exchanges, and the jurisdiction challenge succeeded. Distinguish it
  on prospectus language promising Canadian remedies, or on Canadian operations.
- *Yip v. HSBC Holdings*, 2018 ONCA 626: downloading documents in Ontario is a weak connection.
- *Kamrani-Ghadjar v. Anaergia*, 2025 ONSC 2167: a global IPO class includes purchasers from
  non-Canadian underwriters.
- **CDRs:** OSC decisions on CIBC CDRs (2021 CanLII 63016, paras. 7–10, 31; 2025 decision, paras.
  14–15, "co-owners as tenants-in-common" ⚠ verify the CanLII number: 89253 vs 89235); *Wright v.
  Horizons ETFs*, 2020 ONCA 337. Standing as tenants-in-common: *Cyr v. Ste-Anne de Madawaska*
  (1971 NBCA), *Magnus v. Mason*, 2019 ABQB 341 [Super Micro memos].

### Provincial strategy [TD/First Horizon]
- "Every province would exercise jurisdiction over a shareholder class action cause of action
  against [a reporting issuer]."
- "Common law misrepresentation causes of action can be certified but, if contested, will be
  limited to Canadian investors only." "All provinces will certify the Statutory cause of action
  on a global basis."
- The BC CPA can be relied on only by BC residents. Quebec has a first-to-file rule; Ontario has a
  60-day rule. Quebec may decline a national class unless the defendant is a Quebec corporation.
  *Kowalyshyn v. Valeant*, 2016 ONSC 3819.

## United States (when a US claim or a US parallel action is in play)

**All items in this section are (not from corpus) unless marked: verify before use.**
- §10(b) / Rule 10b-5 elements: (1) a material misrepresentation or omission; (2) scienter; (3) a
  connection with the purchase or sale of a security; (4) reliance; (5) economic loss; (6) loss
  causation. *Stoneridge Investment Partners v. Scientific-Atlanta*, 552 U.S. 148, 157 (2008).
- §11 Securities Act: strict liability for a registration statement (no scienter), with a due
  diligence defence for non-issuers.
- *Morrison v. National Australia Bank*, 561 U.S. 247 (2010): §10(b) reaches only securities listed
  on domestic exchanges and domestic transactions [Super Micro memo]. *Stoyas v. Toshiba*, 896 F.3d
  1001 (9th Cir. 2018) covers unsponsored ADRs [Super Micro memo].
- *Janus Capital v. First Derivative*, 564 U.S. 135 (2011): the "maker" is the one with ultimate
  authority [Super Micro memo].
- Reg S-K Item 303 (known trends and uncertainties) [Gap memo].
- State securities statutes for US-incorporated issuers sued in Canada, e.g. Wis. Stat.
  s. 551.509(2) for breach of s. 551.505 (elements listed in FiServ II).

## United Kingdom and Germany (home-forum or parallel actions)

**All (not from corpus). Andrew's memos treat these forums only as competing or parallel
proceedings. Verify each item before use.**
- UK: FSMA 2000 s. 90A and Schedule 10A. The issuer is liable for untrue or misleading statements
  in published information, or dishonest delay, where a PDMR knew or was reckless; reliance is
  required. FSMA s. 90 covers prospectuses.
- Germany: WpHG §§ 97–98 (formerly §§ 37b–37c), issuer liability for failing to publish, or
  publishing untrue, inside information; KapMuG model-case procedure (Bundesanzeiger register).
  Ad hoc disclosure under MAR Art. 17 (timing of inside information vs publication).
- Australia (appeared in Paladin): a competing class action can absorb most of the float.
  Whether Australia follows *Morrison* was an open question in his memo.
