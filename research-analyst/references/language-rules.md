# Language rules

The memo is research for a lawyer, not an allegation. Write so that every sentence would survive
being read aloud to the company's counsel.

## Never write

| Avoid | Write instead |
|---|---|
| fraud, fraudulent, scheme, lied, deceived, concealed | "did not disclose", "the later disclosure stated…", "appears inconsistent with" |
| misled investors | "investors were told X on [date]; on [date] Y became public" |
| knew (as a conclusion) | "evidence bearing on knowledge includes…" |
| false, untrue (as a conclusion) | "appears inconsistent with [record]" |
| caused the losses, damages | "the price fell X% (abnormal −Y%) on the day the information became public" |
| clearly, obviously, undeniably | state the evidence and let it speak |
| strong case, actionable, liable, viable claim | "warrants further investigation because…" / "no apparent basis because…" |

## Always

- Attribute allegations: "the short-seller report alleges…", "the complaint alleges…".
- Distinguish a court's finding from a party's argument, and a ruling under appeal from a final one.
- Give the date with every statement and every revelation.
- Separate fact, inference and open question visibly (the ledger status drives this).
- When concluding, use the conclusion scale in `memo-writer`.
