# How Andrew researches a drop (distilled from his memos, 2012–2026)

Source: about 60 memos read in full from Andrew Morganti's Drive. They include his own origination
memos (Alamos, Ascot, Aurora, Canacol, FiServ I–II, Global Atomic, Li-Cycle, Paladin, Putnam,
Sintana, Slate Grocery, TD Bank ×2, Terravest, The Gap, VCC, Wayland, Nevsun, Silvercorp), the
memos his associates wrote to him (Endo, Detour, MDC, NAP, Pretium, BP, Colt, Concordia, Super
Micro, HEXO) and the expert damages report in HEXO. `corpus.md` lists each one with its Drive ID.

This file describes how he thinks. `memo-template.md` gives the memo's shape,
`legal-library.md` the law he reuses, `case-types.md` the playbook for each type of case, and
`economics.md` the arithmetic.

## 1. The question he is answering

The lawyer's own question is narrower than the analyst's ("is there an inaccuracy?"):

> **Is there a claim worth issuing? That means a specific prior statement, a corrective
> disclosure linked to it, a price reaction that survives the 10-day look-back, a forum, a class
> big enough to matter against the liability cap, and a defendant with the cash or D&O insurance
> to pay.**

A memo that finds a real misrepresentation but no recoverable class ends in **Pass**. He passed
on Paladin because about 95% of the float traded on the ASX and the Canadian remainder was
$3–5M. A memo with a modest misrepresentation but a big, clean drop and an insured defendant
ends in **Pursue**. Ascot is the example: a "fully funded" claim, a −56% drop on record volume,
and a CEO affidavit contradicting the earlier statements.

## 2. The order in which he works

1. **Measure the drop first.** Close before and after, percentage, volume as a multiple of the
   average ("record volume"), market value lost, and the price ten trading days later. If the
   price recovered within days he says so and usually stops (Alamos: "the stock price immediately
   recovered").
2. **Identify the corrective disclosure precisely.** Use the exact time stamp (after the close →
   next session) and the exact words. Separate a **storm warning** (an early, partial,
   smaller drop) from the **public correction** (Paladin Oct 28 vs Nov 11; Global Atomic
   July 16, 2024 vs Jan 23, 2025). Analyst and media pieces can be corrections too (Aurora:
   MarketWatch, Jefferies).
3. **Profile the issuer.** Name and defined term, incorporating statute and province or state,
   head office, every listing and ticker (TSX/TSXV/CSE/Cboe Canada/NEO, NYSE/Nasdaq, OTCQX,
   Frankfurt, ASX), CUSIP/ISIN, the provinces where it is a reporting issuer, the company's
   self-description quoted, shares outstanding, the market cap range over the class period, key
   assets and subsidiaries, insiders and related parties.
4. **Build the total mix of information.** Use every core document (AIF, MD&A, financial
   statements, prospectus or offering memorandum, material change reports, 10-K/10-Q) and
   non-core document (news releases, calls including analyst Q&A, presentations, ESG reports)
   from roughly a year before the class period to the correction. Write each one as "On [date],
   [TICKER] published its [document] and reported, in relevant part:" followed by verbatim
   quotes with pinpoint footnotes. **Log the price move for each disclosure**, including
   "(nil%)".
5. **Track how the story drifted.** List every date the company promised and every guidance
   figure, and show how each changed. Flag silent changes ("CURIOUS. The date casually changes
   from Q3 to the second half of 2024 without reason"). Watch for reassurances that disappear,
   material change reports that were never filed, and lawsuits or liens that went
   unmentioned in the MD&A.
6. **Look for proof outside the issuer.** This is where his memos are strongest.
   - Sworn evidence: a CEO's affidavit in a later court proceeding (Ascot), CCAA monitor reports
     (Wayland, Canacol, Chesswood).
   - Public registries: builders' liens (Ascot).
   - Government and regulator records: DFC board agendas, State Department, FOIA (Global
     Atomic); AMF records obtained by journalists (Terravest); provincial fire bulletins (Alamos).
   - Counterparty and peer disclosures; foreign press (Canacol / El Colombiano).
   - Plaintiff complaints and confidential-witness allegations in a parallel US action (FiServ,
     Aurora, Endo).
   - Post-correction admissions at conferences, plus analyst notes saying a fact "had not
     previously been disclosed" (FiServ).
7. **Gather evidence that management knew** (a theme that appears in every memo). Look for
   insider sales before the news (SEDI, Form 4; the Li-Cycle CEO's US$13M sale; "as if certain
   market participants knew"), trading that ran ahead of the release, auditor changes (Canacol:
   KPMG → PwC), executive or audit-committee departures, earlier incidents of the same kind
   (Barrick's spills), the timing of the issuer's own excuse (Global Atomic: the "Trump
   election" excuse post-dated statements made after the election), and how long the reporting
   period had run when guidance was reaffirmed.
8. **Frame the claim.** Choose the forum, the causes of action, one class definition for each
   cause of action, the class period ends tied to specific documents, the impugned documents
   (keep the set lean: "referring documents beyond the quarter MD&A and prospectus/offering
   memoranda are unnecessary and only create additional work at the s. 138.3 OSA hearing"), and
   the defendants (issuer plus certifying CEO/CFO, then directors, auditors and underwriters
   where facts support it).
9. **Do the arithmetic.** Damaged shares, the per-share drop and the 10-day look-back, statutory
   versus common-law damages, the 5% liability cap, a settlement target, and D&O insurance plus
   cash.
10. **Argue against himself (Bear v. Bull).** Look for adverse precedent on the same theory
    (Coffin v. Atlantic Power for dividend cuts; Smith v. Gap / Peloton for retail inventory),
    defences (forward-looking safe harbour, s. 138.4(6) reasonable investigation, act of state,
    causation breaks), jurisdiction and standing attacks, rival plaintiff firms and carriage
    fights, and firm capacity. Then explain how this case is different.
11. **Decide and stage it.** The options are Pursue (issue the claim), Pursue but hold (issue
    and wait for the US/foreign mediation), Monitor (name the next catalyst and its date), or
    Pass (give the reason). End with the next steps and the deadlines: the s. 138.14 three-year
    limit, the two-year limit for the common law, and when to serve the leave motion so the
    limitation stops running.

## 4. His decision rules (with the cases they came from)

| Result | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass** | Recoverable class too small after carve-outs (foreign float, competing foreign action); Canadian remainder about $5M or less | Paladin (95% ASX; $3–5M) |
| **Pass** | Class period of only a few months **and** a small float | Paladin |
| **Pass / Monitor** | No lasting price effect (recovered within days; a correction of less than $1 recovered in one day) | Alamos |
| **Monitor** | Facts not settled yet (no regulator report, an investigation still open); name the next catalyst and its date | Alamos (Q3 MD&A due Oct 29) |
| **Monitor** | Something broke the chain of causation (an intervening act by a third party) | Alamos (the Province's "under control" announcement, then the fire reignited) |
| **Pass (for now)** | Crowded field (4+ competing proceedings), well-funded defence, high cost exposure, not enough firm capacity | TD derivative |
| **Pursue** | Sharp drop on record volume, a clear link, **plus** sworn or third-party evidence contradicting the earlier statements | Ascot, Canacol, Global Atomic |
| **Pursue** | Damages meaningful relative to the 5% cap (common-law damages run above the cap); settlement target of about C$5M or more | Ascot (target C$5–11M), Canacol (~$100M) |
| **Pursue, but hold** | A parallel US or foreign class action exists: issue in Ontario, then sit until that action mediates and the Canadian class is invited in | FiServ; Paladin fallback ("issue it and sit on it for 10 months") |
| **Flag as attractive** | Abnormal trading before the release, insider sales or tipping | Sintana, Terravest, Li-Cycle |

There is no explicit market-cap floor. In practice, he looks for a Canadian recoverable class of
roughly $5M or more, and the settlement targets he cites start at about C$5M.

## 5. How he writes

- **First person, as a submission.** "It is my submission that…", "I recognize that there are
  tangible concerns that…", "I recommend against allocating further resources…". Each memo makes
  an argument and ends in a recommendation.
- **Signature terms:** total mix of information; Impugned Documents; Public Corrective Disclosure
  / Statement; core / non-core document; "The market's reaction was harsh and immediate";
  "record volume"; "in relevant part"; "ten-day look-back"; STORM WARNING; CURIOUS;
  "[Emphasis added]"; "issue the claim"; "take a pass"; "allocate resources".
- **Defined terms** are introduced in parentheses and quotes, e.g. ("SGR"), (the "Class
  Period"), (the "US Class Proceeding"). Longer memos end with a Defined Terms glossary.
- **Pinpoint footnotes for everything:** document, date, page or paragraph, link; transcript
  speaker and the analyst who asked.
- **Bracketed working notes inside quotes:** "[No reference to lack of ore to mill]".

The plugin keeps his structure and his terms. It applies two disciplines his drafts did not
always have, because his own memos show the cost of their absence:
1. Every number is computed by a script and tied to a ledger record. His memos contain
   arithmetic slips: −44.2% written for −30.7%, "66%" for about 46%, and a 5% cap that mixed
   USD and CAD.
2. Text is never carried over from another case. Several of his memos (Sintana, Terravest,
   Putnam, Canacol, TD/First Horizon) contain paragraphs from other matters with the wrong
   ticker. `scripts/ledger.py memo` checks for this.

## 6. Where jurisdiction matters, and where it does not

The method is the same for any issuer: measure, find the correction, build the total mix, look
for outside proof and knowledge, frame, quantify, argue both sides, decide. Only the **legal
module** changes with jurisdiction:
- The forum and its statute (Ontario OSA Part XXIII.1 / other provinces, US §10(b) and §11,
  UK FSMA s. 90A, Germany WpHG §§ 97–98 / KapMuG).
- The liability cap and damages window.
- Leave and certification hurdles.
- Limitation periods.

Andrew's practice looks at every issuer from the forums available to the firm. The first
question is always whether a Canadian claim exists: reporting issuer, responsible issuer, CDRs,
or Canadian holders of US-listed shares. A US claim comes next, through the firm's US colleagues
or a parallel action. A claim in the issuer's home country (UK, Germany, Australia) matters
mainly as a competing or parallel proceeding that can shrink or crowd out the Canadian class.
`jurisdictions.md` sets out the forum checklist.

## 7. What his memos do not do that the plugin adds

- Abnormal returns against the market and peers. He often uses raw close-to-close changes; the
  plugin reports both and uses the abnormal return to answer "was it the market?".
- A limitations clock in every memo (he computed one only in TD).
- An insider-trading pull (SEDI / Form 4) in every memo, not just some.
- A Bear v. Bull section in every memo. It appears only in his 2026 memos, but it is how he
  thinks: Coffin in Slate, the Peloton notes in Gap, "tangible concerns" everywhere.
- A matrix linking each impugned document to each misrepresentation and each correction. This
  is the s. 138.3 linkage, which he did by hand in Ascot and Global Atomic.
- Fields ready for the firm's pipeline: CUSIP/ISIN, stage, next step, deadlines (the BM.ca
  pipeline memo format).
