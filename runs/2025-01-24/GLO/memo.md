Attorney Work-Product

From: Andrew Morganti

To: File

Re: In re Global Atomic Corporation ("GLO") Securities Litigation

Preamble

An Issuer reports that in order for it to build its flagship mining project it required project debt from a government development bank. The Issuer reported, quarter after quarter, that the bank had authorized the financing to proceed, that the bank's credit committee and board would approve the loan on dated schedules, and finally that it was in the final stages of approval and had answered all of the bank's geo-political concerns. None of the dated approvals occurred, and the Issuer then told the market that the major component of its funding would come from either a bank financing or a joint venture, alongside a discounted private placement.

During the Class Period the Issuer reported the dates, the bank's support and its intention to approve, but omitted to disclose why each date was missed, what questions and conditions stood between the application and the bank's credit committee, how the deteriorating relationship between the host country and the bank's government bore on the loan, and that the bank's process had paused before the U.S. presidential election. It is my submission that the status of the application before the bank, the reasons for the serial delays and the pre-conditions of approval were Material Facts and were required to have been disclosed.

Proposed Class Period

Suggested to be November 10, 2023 through January 23, 2025; two public corrections.

Defined Terms

Global Atomic Corporation ("GLO") is a corporation incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (Ontario) on May 27, 1994, with its registered and principal office at 8 King Street East, Suite 1700, Toronto, Ontario; it is a reporting issuer in all provinces and territories of Canada and its common shares are listed on TSX: GLO, OTCQX: GLATF and FRA: G12; ISIN CA37957M1068 and CUSIP 37957M106.[^1]

The Dasa Project ("Dasa") is GLO's uranium project in the Republic of Niger, held through Société Minière de DASA S.A. ("SOMIDA"), which is 80% owned by GLO through Global Atomic Fuels Corporation and 20% by the Government of Niger.[^2]

The U.S. International Development Finance Corporation ("DFC") is the U.S. development bank that GLO referred to during the Class Period as the "U.S. development bank" or the "Bank".[^3]

Stephen G. Roman ("Roman") is GLO's Chairman, President and CEO, a director since 2005 and the speaker of each CEO quotation in the Impugned Documents.[^4]

Rein A. Lehari ("Lehari") is GLO's Chief Financial Officer.[^5]

Red Cloud Securities Inc. ("Red Cloud") was the lead underwriter and sole bookrunner of the October 2024 prospectus offering of units (the "October 2024 Offering").[^6]

The "Class Period" is November 10, 2023 through January 23, 2025.

The "Impugned Documents" are the quarterly and annual MD&A filed during the Class Period, the results news releases filed with them, and the prospectus supplement for the October 2024 Offering.

The "Public Corrective Disclosures" are the news releases issued after the close of trading on July 16, 2024 and January 23, 2025.

Overview

GLO describes itself as "a Toronto-based company that provides a unique combination of cash-flowing zinc concentrate production and high-grade uranium development."[^7] Its investment thesis (total mix of information) was the construction of Dasa, for which the 60% debt portion of the project financing was to come from Export Development Canada and a U.S. development bank.[^8] The U.S. development bank's facility was to be US$295 million, covering 60% of the planned project costs.[^9] GLO's cash was $4.2 million at September 30, 2024, before the October 2024 Offering.[^10]

The DFC's letter of interest dated from June 2022,[^11] and the Dasa environmental and social impact assessment was posted on the DFC's website in February 2023 for the public review tied to GLO's application to Export Development Canada and the DFC.[^12] Throughout the Class Period, GLO presented the DFC loan as the major component of Dasa's construction financing and gave the market dated credit committee and board approvals.

The theme of the causes of action is three-fold:

(a) the status of the application before the DFC, i.e., that the loan had not been placed before the DFC's credit committee or board on any of the dates reported;[^13]

(b) the reasons for each delay and the pre-conditions to approval, including the effect of the breakdown in relations between Niger and the United States on the loan; and

(c) the timing of the DFC's pause, which GLO later admitted began before the 2024 U.S. presidential election, while GLO was reporting that the loan was in its final stages.

The specific misrepresentations that are relevant to the proposed legal proceeding are when GLO negligently reported that:

(a) the DFC's credit committee approval would happen during January 2024,[^14] April 2024,[^15] Q2 2024,[^16] mid-July 2024,[^17] Q4 2024[^18] and early Q1 2025;[^19] and

(b) the DFC board's final approval would happen during March 2024,[^20] June 2024,[^21] Q2 2024,[^22] September 2024,[^23] October 2024,[^24] Q3 2024,[^25] Q4 2024,[^26] early Q1 2025[^27] and Q1 2025.[^28]

Conducting the deep-dive due diligence exercise independent of GLO's disclosures, and primarily at the DFC's own published board records, my conclusion is that the Dasa loan did not come before the DFC board at any board meeting or notational vote in 2024 (March 6, June 5, September 5 and December 4, 2024, and the notational votes through October 28, 2024), and that the DFC's credit committee did not review the loan until about December 2025.[^29]

The Class Period would be November 10, 2023 through January 23, 2025, with two Public Corrective Disclosures (i.e., the final being $0.94 to $0.64).[^30]

PART A. FACTS

Background

On February 24, 2023, GLO posted the Dasa environmental and social impact assessment on the DFC's website for a 60-day public review tied to its application to Export Development Canada and the DFC; the DFC screened Dasa as Category A.[^31]

On October 10, 2023, $2.44 to $1.99 (down 18.4%), GLO reported that the U.S. State Department had designated the July 2023 events in Niger a coup d'état, and that:[^32]

> This designation will put a temporary hold on US Development Bank financing pending visibility of a return to democratic elections.

This is an Act of State and not a public correction; however, from this date the market knew that the DFC loan depended on the relationship between Niger and the United States, and every later statement by GLO about U.S. Government support must be read against it.

The Beginning of the Class Period

On November 10, 2023, $2.05 to $2.13 (increased 3.9%), GLO published its Q3 2023 results and reported that "the U.S. Government has expressed support for the project financing to proceed and both development banks have been authorized to re-engage with the Company and finalize funding arrangements."[^33] The other material facts include:

(a) "With the re-engagement of the development banks, the schedule for project financing is targeted for credit committee approval in January followed by final approval in March 2024.";[^34]

(b) "The Company is well advanced in negotiating a term sheet and expects this to be concluded shortly.";[^35] and

(c) "In view of the uncertainties about the timing of completion of a project financing, the Company has commenced a process to review alternative funding options." [No reference to what the uncertainties were].[^36]

On December 19, 2023, $2.80 to $2.58 (down 7.9%), GLO announced a C$15 million strategic investment by Regent Mercantile and Stephen Dattels, and Roman stated that the investment "coincides with better visibility on timelines associated with democratic elections and the restoration of supply lines".[^37]

On January 10, 2024, $3.08 to $3.19 on the next session (increased 3.6%), GLO reported that with its third offtake agreement it "has satisfied the "pre-sales" requirement of its banking syndicate."[^38]

On March 5, 2024, $3.21 to $3.21 (nil%), GLO reported that "the 60% debt portion of the project financing is expected to be satisfied in Q2 2024" and Roman stated: "We expect to have full project financing in place soon".[^39]

On March 16, 2024, Niger announced the end of its military cooperation with the United States. On March 18, 2024, $3.13 down to $2.17 (down 30.7%) on volume 6.9 times the 30-day average, GLO responded that "in addition to current advanced discussions with project lenders, the Company is also pursuing other financing strategies".[^40] This is an Act of State and not a public correction; however, it was later reported that Niger's decision followed a mid-March meeting at which senior U.S. officials raised "reports of Iran seeking raw materials in the country, including uranium."[^41]

On March 27, 2024, $2.20 to $2.27 on the next session (increased 3.2%), GLO published its 2023 MD&A and reported that "The Company has been advised by this banking syndicate that Credit Committee approval may occur in April 2024, followed by final approval by the Board of Directors in June 2024."[^42] The other material facts include:

(a) on Niger's termination of military cooperation: "Global Atomic understands the two countries are in discussions to reach a mutually acceptable resolution." [No reference to the effect on the DFC loan];[^43] and

On May 19, 2024, Niger and the U.S. Department of Defense announced that U.S. troops would withdraw from Niger by September 15, 2024.[^46] GLO's MD&A through Q3 2024 does not discuss the effect of the withdrawal on the DFC loan.[^47]

On June 17, 2024, $2.00 to $2.09 (increased 4.5%), GLO reported that the bank "is completing the final documentation to submit to its Credit Committee for approval, which has now been scheduled to take place in mid-July 2024", that it "expects final review by the bank's Board of Directors in September", and Roman stated: "We believe the U.S. Government recognizes the significance of the Dasa Project".[^48]

On July 16, 2024, after the close, $2.02 down to $1.67 (down 17.3%) on volume of 2.0 million shares (4.5 times the 30-day average) being traded on the TSX on July 17, 2024, GLO reported that "The Bank decided not to bring this project to the Credit Committee in July, citing a need to answer and confirm additional questions" and that "final approval by the Bank's Board of Directors is now expected in October 2024."[^49]

PART B. ISSUES

Did GLO release statements that negligently omitted material facts about the status, timing and pre-conditions of the DFC loan for Dasa in breach of common law and the Ontario Securities Act ("OSA"):

(a) that the DFC had not scheduled, and did not hold, the credit committee and board approvals on the dates GLO reported, and why; and

(b) that the DFC's questions and pause concerned matters, including the relationship between Niger and the United States, that GLO described as answered and as in their final stages?

The affirmative defences will be that each date was attributed to the bank and carried cautionary language, that the July 16, 2024 postponement was disclosed promptly, that the coup designation and the U.S. withdrawal were Acts of State known to the market, that the 2024 election and the change of administration were new events, and that the DFC did approve the loan in September 2026.[^70] GLO will also rely on the reasonable investigation defence and on Mask v. Silvercorp for forecast-type statements.[^71] Framing the claim on GLO's own statements of present fact (the bank "confirmed", "final stages", "answered all geo-political concerns") and on the omission of the reasons for each missed date, rather than on the geopolitical events themselves, neutralizes the Act of State defence; the 2026 approval is subject to conditions and came twenty months after the final correction.

PART C. RULES ~ LAW

The Causes of Action

The causes of action would be primary and secondary market negligent misrepresentations:[^72]

(a) The Impugned Offerings for the primary market causes of action occurred on October 16, 2024 (the October 2024 Offering, with Red Cloud as underwriter).

(b) The Class Period for the secondary market causes of action would be November 10, 2023 through January 23, 2025.

The Rules and Laws

The relevant rules and laws are:

(a) Common law. The common law misrepresentation cause of action for negligent misrepresentation are:

(i) Duty of care based on a special relationship between the plaintiff and the defendant;

(ii) An untrue, inaccurate, or misleading representation;

(iii) The defendant making the representation negligently;

(iv) The plaintiff having reasonably relied on the misrepresentation;[^73] and,

(v) The plaintiff suffered damages as a consequence of relying on the misrepresentation.

(b) Securities Act, Part XXIII, s. 130 primary market; and

(c) Securities Act, Part XXIII.1, s. 138.3 secondary market, if leave is granted, the constituent elements of the statutory cause of action are:

(i) the making of a misrepresentation or the failure to disclose a material fact;

(ii) a public correction of the false information; and

(iii) the trading of a security between the time of the misrepresentation or the failure to disclose a material fact.

To plead the statutory causes of action, the plaintiff should: (1) identify the inculpatory statement or omission and when it was made or ought to have been made; (2) specify the falseness of the inculpatory statement; and (3) identify the public correction and when it was made.

While reliance would not be a constituent element of the cause of action, there is a liability limit measured by 5% of the Company's market capitalization at the commencement of the release of the first or each of the Impugned Documents.[^74]

PART D. ANALYSIS

The core theme of the misrepresentations across all of the Impugned Documents is that GLO consistently omitted material facts about the status of its application before the DFC (e.g., that no credit committee date had been fixed, why each date was missed, and what questions remained, the same theme as Li-Cycle):

(a) First, from November 10, 2023 to November 12, 2024, GLO gave the market a credit committee date or a board date in every quarter, and moved it every quarter: January and March 2024, then April and June 2024, then Q2 2024, then mid-July and September 2024, then October 2024, then Q3 and Q4 2024 in two documents released the same day, then early Q1 2025.[^75] None of those approvals occurred, the DFC board did not consider Dasa at any 2024 meeting, and the credit committee first reviewed the loan in about December 2025.[^76] Only once, on July 16, 2024, did GLO give a reason ("additional questions"), and it never said what the questions were. It is my submission that the reasons for each delay and the status of the application were material facts. If they are material facts GLO had an obligation to disclose them so investors could make informed decisions about their investment in GLO.

(b) Second, after the July 2024 postponement GLO did not retreat to forecasts; it escalated to statements of present fact. On October 29, 2024 the bank had "confirmed" its intention to approve; on November 12, 2024 Dasa was "in the final stages of obtaining approval" and GLO had "answered all geo-political concerns raised by the bank".[^77] On March 27, 2025, GLO disclosed that the bank's process had paused "before and after" the November 5, 2024 election, which places the pause over those same statements; on December 17, 2024 GLO had attributed the delay to the "recent" election only after the fact.[^78] It is my submission that the pause, and the questions that remained before the bank, were material facts. If they are material facts GLO had an obligation to disclose them so investors could make informed decisions about their investment in GLO.

(c) Third, GLO continued to report U.S. Government support while the relationship between Niger and the United States broke down: the coup designation of October 2023, the termination of military cooperation in March 2024 after U.S. officials raised concerns about uranium, and the agreed withdrawal of U.S. forces by September 15, 2024.[^79] The 2026 approval was made subject to conditions on an export route, the mining convention, repayment assurances and a direct agreement with Niger.[^80] It is my submission that the effect of these events on the loan was a material fact. If it is a material fact GLO had an obligation to disclose it so investors could make informed decisions about their investment in GLO.

The Class Period starts on November 10, 2023, the first document reporting that the banks were authorized to "finalize funding arrangements" and the first dated schedule; an alternative start is March 27, 2024, the 2023 MD&A, the first core document with dated credit committee and board months.[^81] It is my submission that referring documents beyond the quarter MD&A and prospectus/offering memoranda are unnecessary and only create additional work at the s. 138.3 OSA submission/hearing (e.g., the "Impugned Documents"). The October 29 and December 17, 2024 news releases are pleaded as part of the total mix of information.

For each Impugned Document, the allegation would be:

This statement contained a misrepresentation because it omitted the material facts of:

(a) the status of GLO's application before the DFC, including that no credit committee or board date had been fixed by the DFC on the dates reported;

(b) the reasons each reported date was missed, including the questions the DFC required GLO to answer and the pause in the DFC's process before and after the 2024 U.S. presidential election; and

(c) the effect of the breakdown in relations between Niger and the United States on the DFC loan, and the pre-conditions of the DFC's approval.

For each of the Public Corrective Statements, the allegation to link the statements to the Impugned Documents would be:

(a) On July 16, 2024, $2.02 down to $1.67 (down 17.3%) on the next session, GLO reported that the bank decided not to bring Dasa to its July credit committee, citing a need to answer and confirm additional questions.[^82]

(b) The importance of the July 16, 2024 disclosure is that it was the first time GLO told the market that a reported date was missed because of the bank's unanswered questions, against the June 17, 2024 report that final documentation was being completed for a scheduled credit committee.

(c) On January 23, 2025, $0.94 down to $0.64 (down 31.9%) on the next session, GLO reported that the major component of Dasa's funding would come as "either a bank financing or a joint venture agreement", together with a discounted, fully warranted placement.[^83]

(d) The importance of the January 23, 2025 disclosure is that the DFC loan, which GLO had reported as confirmed and in its final stages with approval by early Q1 2025, was now one of two alternatives three weeks into that quarter. The market's reaction was harsh and immediate: the abnormal return was −31.3% with no recovery over the ten-day look-back.[^84]

PART E. CONCLUSION

At the commencement of the Class Period GLO had 202,253,352 common shares outstanding.[^85] GLO's market capitalization was $407.3 million, using the average closing price of $2.014 over the ten trading days before November 10, 2023.[^86]

On July 17, 2024, GLO's share price closed at $1.67 from $2.02 (down 17.3%), on 2,044,704 share volume just on the TSX, alone. By July 30, 2024, the ten-day look-back, GLO's share price on the TSX was at $1.29, for a maximum of $0.73 per share for any investor that purchased shares during the Class Period and held them beyond the tenth-trading day.[^87]

On January 24, 2025, GLO's share price closed at $0.64 from $0.94 (down 31.9%), on 14,400,673 share volume just on the TSX, alone. By February 6, 2025, the ten-day look-back, GLO's share price on the TSX was at $0.53, for a maximum of $0.41 per share for any investor that purchased shares during the Class Period and held them beyond the tenth-trading day.[^88]

During the Class Period 260,208,469 GLO shares traded on the TSX, about 73% of the volume across the TSX, OTCQX and Frankfurt. Rough common law damages are about $92.0 million (range $30.9 million to $115.0 million) on the TSX alone, and the statutory claim would be limited by the 5% cap to $20.4 million. The primary market claim on the October 2024 Offering is about $20.2 million ($1.20 less the $0.597 ten-day look-back average, on 33,542,050 units). The s. 138.14 limitation for the first Impugned Document runs on November 10, 2026; the notice of motion for leave should be served with the claim.[^89]

I believe that the facts and quantum of damages justify an investor to lodge a claim against GLO and Roman, Chairman, President and CEO, and Lehari, Chief Financial Officer, who certified each of the Impugned Documents.[^90]
