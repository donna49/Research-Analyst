Project Description
The proceeds of the DFC loan will finance the development and construction of a greenfield
underground uranium mine and first stage ore converter located in north-central Niger. The
Project will produce triuranium octoxide (“yellowcake”) that is the feedstock for uranium
conversion and enrichment, yielding fuel for nuclear power generation.

Policy Review
Developmental Objectives
Niger’s economy is poorly diversified, with agriculture accounting for 34% of its GDP and 70%
of working age employment. Mining is another important contributor to the country’s GDP and,
prior to the coup d’état in 2023, uranium was the country’s second largest export. In 2021, one of
the country’s two uranium mines, Compagnie Minière d’Akokan (“COMINAK”), closed
because of resource depletion and consequentially reduced the country’s export revenue, mining
royalties, and employment opportunities. In response to these challenges, the Project will support
the development, construction, and operation of a new mine that is expected to produce
approximately four million pounds of uranium annually for export under long-term contracts to
utilities in North America, Europe, and Asia for use in nuclear power generation. The Project is
expected to result in more than eight billion dollars of uranium export sales over the life of the
mine, generating significant local income in Niger and new, full-time jobs in the mining
industry. Given the Project’s characteristics, it is categorized as Highly Impactful per DFC’s
Impact Quotient.
The Project involves the mining of uranium at a site which is located near Agadez in northcentral Niger. The surrounding areas are sparsely inhabited with no permanent villages within

the Project’s direct (i.e., 7 km) Area of Influence (AOI). Livestock grazing occurs to a limited
extent within the area outside the direct AOI.
SCREENING:
The Project has been reviewed against DFC’s categorical prohibitions and determined to be
categorically eligible. The Project was screened as Category A because of the significant diverse
impacts that are associated with the extraction of uranium from an underground mine and
processing of the extracted ore.
APPLICABLE STANDARDS:
DFC’s due diligence indicates that the Project will have impacts that must be managed in a
manner consistent with the following International Finance Corporation’s (IFC) 2012
Performance Standards (PS):
- PS 1: Assessment and Management of Environmental and Social Risks and Impacts;
- PS 2: Labor and Working Conditions;
- PS 3: Resource Efficiency and Pollution Prevention;
- PS 4: Community Health, Safety, and Security;
- PS 6: Biodiversity Conservation and Sustainable Management of Living Natural
Resources;
Since the time of DFC’s disclosure of the Project impact assessment in April 2023, PS 5 on Land
Acquisition and Involuntary Resettlement, PS 7 on Indigenous Peoples and PS 8 on Cultural
Heritage were not triggered. There is no anticipated physical or economic displacement
associated with this Project, so PS 5 is not triggered at this time. While the screening highlighted
the existence of Indigenous Peoples (Tuareg), engagement with community elders determined
their Indigenous practices and livelihood would not be impacted by the Project, and the risks and
impacts to this community are adequately managed under PS 1, so PS 7 is not triggered.
Similarly, while the screening found evidence of some cultural and archaeological heritage in
some of the neighboring villages, it found no cultural heritage materials within the mining permit
area. Impact screening concluded that construction activities are unlikely to encounter replicable,
non-replicable, or critical cultural heritage. The Project will develop and implement a Chance
Finds Procedure as a risk management approach, so PS 8 is not triggered at this time.
In accordance with PS 3, the following sector-specific IFC Guidelines apply to the Project:
Mining Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) (2007); General (2007); Ports, Harbors, and
Terminals (2007); Waste Management (2007), and Water and Sanitation (2007).
POLICY RISKS AND MITIGATION:
The Project will update its management systems as a condition of DFC financing. The project’s
existing management systems are modelled after ISO 14001 and cover the construction phase.
DFC will require amended management systems as necessary to align with international
standards and Good International Industry Practices (GIIP) and also to carry the Project into the
operational phase and through the closure phase.
The following summarizes key areas of risk and project mitigation measures:

Mine Safety
Stability analysis of the entire mine system has been undertaken and safety considerations have
been incorporated in the design which include availability of fire detection, suppression, and
protection equipment (particularly in areas where rubber-tired vehicles are present); a robust
ventilation system coupled with flammable gas testing and warning; temperature monitoring and
air conditioning in vehicles to protect against heat stress; and escape and rescue provisions. A
consultant has reviewed mine safety and found it to be in conformance with the International
Council of Mining and Metals (ICMM) Guidelines.
Radiation Protection
The overall aim of Dasa’s radiation protection procedures is to ensure that all equipment,
processes, work methods and operations are designed and operated so that individual and
collective exposures are kept as low as reasonably achievable (ALAR) and below the regulatory
limits and international standards. There are three general approaches used to minimize exposure
to radiation: maximize the distance between the source and the worker; limit exposure times; and
place shielding between the worker and the source.
Exposure can occur via the inhalation of very fine ore or uranate dust (radioactive aerosols),
particularly the short-lived alpha emitters radon-222 and radon-220, and long-lived alpha
emitters from ore dust. Protection against these is affected by limiting the airborne content of the
products (through capture and water suppression) or by rapidly evacuating the particles in
suspension (ventilation). The wearing of individual protection (masks, work clothes) is also
required for collective protection.
As part of comprehensive occupational health and safety planning for the Project, a Radiation
Management Plan has been developed, which is in accordance with the International Atomic
Energy Agency (IAEA)/International Council on Radiation Protection (ICRP) Guidelines.
Workplaces will be regularly (continuously) monitored to maintain acceptable exposure dose
rates, and short- and long-lived emitters. Reference guideline values will be set at various facility
locations for radon, gamma radiation, ore dust, and uranate dust. If any measurement exceeds
these reference values (which have been set to protect personnel working in underground
uranium mines by the IAEA), work will be stopped until safe conditions resume. Personnel
considered to be at risk of high exposure will wear an integrated multi-risk dosimeter and will
undergo regular medical surveillance.
To minimize radiation exposure to the workforce, the air in the active ore mining areas will be
replaced every three to four minutes. This will be achieved by a ventilation system which will
draw air into the mine at 400 cubic meters per second (m3/s). The retention time of the air
flowing through the general mine will not exceed 15 minutes.
In addition to the mine site and occupational radiation monitoring, Dasa will undertake
radiological monitoring of the ambient air quality around the mine site and nearby communities
to monitor potential impacts on the population and the environment. An additional program is
being developed to monitor potential radiological impacts to groundwater, soil, and the food
chain in the wider area.

Water Resources
There are no permanent water courses in the Project area. However, the kori channels are
characterized by short duration, high flow events in response to heavy rainfall. They remain dry
for most of the year, but flash floods can occur as a result of local storm events.
Groundwater in the Project area includes an alternating sequence of high- to moderatepermeability sandstones and low-permeability siltstones and mudstones. The shallow Tchirézrine
aquifers occur at less than 80 meters in depth and are important for local water supplies, whereas
the deeper Teloua and Tarat units are considered regional aquifers.
Groundwater modelling has predicted that inflow of groundwater to the mine will be
significantly greater than the total Project water demand, including what is needed for ore
processing and domestic needs. This groundwater must be removed to allow mining to progress
safely. This results in a risk of natural groundwater levels in the surrounding area being drawn
down. Modelling suggests that by the end of the 12-year mine life, water levels in wells in
Tagaza and Eghatrak may decline by around 2 meters (m) as a result of mine dewatering,
whereas at the market gardens of Elagozan (5 km south of the mine), the decline may be 10 m or
more.
Soil and groundwater resources in the Project area will be protected through the implementation
of GIIPs for materials and waste handling, including the development of a Hazardous Materials
Management Plan, a Spill Response Plan (part of the Emergency Preparedness and Response
Plan), a Tailings Management Plan (for dry tailings in accordance with the Global Industry
Standard on Tailings Management [GISTM]), a Waste Management Plan, and a Wastewater
Management Plan). Wastewaters will be reused after treatment and there will be no effluent
discharges.
Any impact to local wells would be gradual, and detectable by appropriate monitoring, thus
enabling early planning of appropriate mitigation measures (e.g., provision of alternative water
supplies, lowering the pump head in the existing wells, deepening the existing wells, or installing
a replacement deeper well). As per IFC PS3, the Project will implement sustainable natural
resources management plans, including those for water (in Water Management Plan). Dasa is
investigating strategies to reduce the inflow of groundwater to the mine, in order to reduce the
requirement for dewatering and to minimize water requirements. Such strategies should also
lower the risk of significant drawdowns in local community wells. The strategies under
consideration are a combination of targeted grouting (to block water inflows) and dewatering
(via boreholes). There is also a proposal to extract water up-gradient of the mine and re-inject it
down-gradient.
Dust and Air Emissions, and Noise
Control of dust will be of critical importance during all Project phases. A Dust Management Plan
will be implemented (as part of the Air Quality and Greenhouse Gas Emissions Management
Plan) and will be based upon the strategies outlined in the IFC’s Mining and General Guidelines.

The Project will employ GIIPs for the control of air emissions and noise in accordance with the
IFC EHS General and Mining Guidelines and will be required to maintain acceptable ambient air
quality levels in compliance with the IFC Guidelines.
Wastewater
Dasa will procure a packaged wastewater treatment unit whose specifications will include
compliance with the IFC’s General and Mining Guidelines. Treated wastewaters will be reused
in the mining operations including dust control and there will be no discharges of wastewaters.
Prevailing arid conditions limit the possibility of any runoff.
Resource Conservation
In line with IFC PS3, Dasa will continuously seek and implement cost effective measures for
improving efficiency in its energy usage, as well as conserve water and other natural resources
and material inputs.
Biodiversity
Field surveys and GIS analysis have confirmed that areas of perennial vegetation exist in
association with koris and in lowland areas. The extent of vegetation cover is similar across both
the dry and wet seasons. A total of 38 floral species have been identified in the area, including
five woody species which are protected under Nigerien law.
Recent surveys recorded a total of 54 faunal species in the area. Large mammals included
Fennec, Dorcas gazelle, Aoudad (barbary sheep), Patas monkey, African wildcat, and Golden
jackal. Of these, Dorcas gazelle and Aoudad are listed as ‘Vulnerable’ on the International Union
for the Conservation of Nature’s (IUCN) Red List of Threatened Species. Of the seven reptiles
recorded, one, the Spiny-tailed lizard, is listed as ‘Near-Threatened’ on the IUCN Red List.
Bird species observed during the surveys include Egyptian vulture and Lappet-faced vulture,
both of which are listed as ‘Endangered’ by IUCN; and Tawny eagle, which is listed as
‘Vulnerable’.
No ‘critical habitat’, per IFC’s PS6 definition, has been identified in the Project’s Area of
Influence (AOI). This conclusion is based on GIS spatial assessment, desktop critical habitat
screening, and historical and recent fieldwork. The species of conservation concern that are
present in the general area are considered unlikely to trigger a critical habitat determination
because they are all wide-ranging and the Project is not expected to have any significant impact.
Any residual impacts will be managed in the Biodiversity Action Plan which will ensure
compliance with PS 6.
Cumulative Impacts
The wider region around the Project is an established uranium mining center. Although the
COMINAK mine near Arlit (about 110 km northwest of Dasa) closed in March 2021, the Société
des Mines de l’Aïr (“SOMAÏR”) mine, also located near Arlit, is currently operating and
expected to do so until 2035. The Madouela project, for which a mining feasibility study was
published in 2022, is located immediately southeast of Arlit. Approximately 50 km west of Dasa
is the Imouraren deposit, for which an operating permit was awarded in 2009 but whose
development has been on hold since 2015, pending an improvement in market conditions. The

relatively large distances between the various existing and potential future developments are
such that cumulative impacts on ambient environmental conditions are unlikely to add
significantly to the impacts already identified for Dasa.
Should both the Imouraren and Madouela projects come on stream during the Dasa project’s life,
impacts may become significant. These may include common pressures associated with influx of
workers, including inflation of the local economy, overwhelming of local infrastructure and
services, over-depletion of natural resources, and loss of traditional cultural heritage and ways of
life. Dasa will develop management plans to maintain Project impacts to acceptable levels in
compliance with the IFC’s Performance Standards.
Community Safety and Security Management
Although the Project site is relatively remote, there are limited potential health and safety risks to
the local population especially from possible accidents relating to the movement of Projectrelated vehicles on public roads and through communities (including transportation of hazardous
materials such as explosives, chemicals, and uranium products); reduced availability or
contamination of water supplies; risk of physical harm to pastoralists or other community
members present nearer to the Project site; and risk of increased disease transmission from
Project staff interacting with the local communities (although staff will be housed in a workers’
camp which would be required to conform to IFC/EBRD’s Guidance Note and no significant
interactions with the local communities, which are located at a considerable distance, is
expected).
Dasa will develop and implement a Community Health, Safety and Security Management Plan
(CHSSMP) which will be in accordance with PS4, the IFC EHS General Guidelines, and the
United Nations Environment Program’s Awareness and Preparedness for Emergencies at Local
Level (APELL) standards. The Voluntary Principles on Security and Human Rights (VPSHR)
will also be addressed in the CHSSP.

Stakeholder Engagement
Although the Project is expected to bring significant benefits to the local area in terms of direct
and indirect employment opportunities and incomes in general, stakeholder engagement has
raised concerns over local traditions and customs potentially being lost, as a result of an
incoming workforce and a switch to mining-related livelihoods. This risk will be lowered by the
Project having its own, self-contained accommodation camp located at a distance from the local
villages. Dasa will formulate a plan to raise awareness among staff and subcontractors about
respecting the traditional practices and customs of the local population. A Code of Conduct will
be drawn up to encourage respectful interaction with the local communities. Camp residents will
be discouraged from entering local communities for recreational purposes.
Site Visit:
DFC’s due diligence included a site visit during the week of January 15, 2023. In addition to
visiting existing and planned Project sites around Agadez, meetings were held with the Project’s
health and safety team, including the community relations department; national regulators in
Niamey that review impact assessments, including an agency focused on protected areas and
biodiversity; thermal power plant (located 20 kms from the mine) which has been designated to
supply power to the grid to support the mine; and representatives of the Agadez community.
